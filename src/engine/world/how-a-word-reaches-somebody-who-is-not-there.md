# How a word reaches somebody who is not there

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="somebody sends word to their house or to a person, cuts slips, or asks what a pair of communication jade does" -->

There are two instruments and the difference between them is who is at the far
end. Each is written up fully beside its own code; what was missing was the one
page that says which is which, so this is that page and it restates neither.

**A communication talisman goes to a HALL.** Counted rather than tracked,
because they are common and single use; marked with a house; keyed to the person
carrying it, so burning one sends word AS them; cut in pairs, with the twin kept
in the hall where the house's lamps burn. A slip burnt anywhere arrives at its
twin and is read there by a person, because a house cannot receive a message.
It has a reach, and a hall further off than a slip carries is a refusal that says
so. Nothing comes back on it: an answer reaches you when you are at the house or
somebody walks it to you.

**A pair of communication jade goes to a PERSON.** Two tracked halves naming each
other, one held by each of two people, spent by nobody and usable as often as
either wants, wherever the two of them happen to be. Masters and elders have
them; a master gives one half to a disciple they value and keeps the twin. It
carries word and not position - holding a half tells you nothing about where the
other half is standing, and nothing in the engine reads a location off one.

**Both are an identity, so both break with the lamp.** *"They break when your ID
and life lamp break"*: a person's slips and their halves of a pair go when their
token and life lamp do, at death and on leaving the house that keyed them. A
missing person's lamp still burns and their slips still work.

**Who may cut them** is two questions with two answers. Their own: anybody at
Foundation or above, marked with their own house, so somebody on no roll cuts
none - a mark is whose it is. The HOUSE's own blanks are an office's work rather
than a favour anybody may do it, and a member who has not been handed the seal is
told so and told who has (`who-cuts-the-houses-slips.ts`).

## Never bare *jade*

A **jade tag** is already a thing in this world: the token cut with a house's
name that says whose you are (`docs/world/houses/trust.md`). It is not this.

So ours is never *jade* on its own. It is **a pair of communication jade**, or
**a jade pair** where the sentence has already said which, written out in full
every time - in prose the player reads, in comments, and in any new symbol.
Where an existing symbol is bare, it is bare because it was written before this
rule and not because there are two meanings in play.
