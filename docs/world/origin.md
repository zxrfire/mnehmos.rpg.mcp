## Birth is the third dealt thing

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="a character is being made, or somebody's birth is raised as an explanation" -->

A cultivator is dealt a spirit root, four attributes, and **a place to be born into**. The
first two decide what they could become. The third decides whether they will ever be in a
position to find out.

Almost everyone is born to nothing: a farm in a thin county, a family with no name outside
its own valley, no teacher, no manual, no vein, and no one to vouch for them. That is the
default and it should stay the default.

A vanishing few are born somewhere that changes the arithmetic.

## What privilege actually buys

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="a well-born cultivator's advantages are weighed, or somebody expects birth to buy rank" -->

It does not buy rank, and it must never buy rank. The great institutions admit on what you
have reached, not on whose child you are - the Hollow Court's bar is a Void Refinement
floor and evidence you could cross, and *nothing else counts, which explicitly includes
being somebody's child.*

What it buys is **inputs**, and inputs are what the ladder actually runs on:

- **Resources on a scale that changes outcomes.** Pills a poor cultivator will never hold.
  A cave on a real vein instead of a thin hillside. Provisions that make a forty-year
  seclusion a plan rather than a way to starve.
- **Placement, which is not the same as a name.** Not admission to the top, and *not a good
  allied sect either, on the name alone.* A house admits on a stated ordinal, a child has
  none until they have cultivated, and the two facts together mean the houses a great
  family's name reaches at the age its children are placed are exactly the ones that admit
  at the floor - the houses that would have taken a farmer's child the same morning. The
  name buys the conversation. What buys the door is the next bullet, and without it the
  whole of this one collapses into an afternoon's walk.
- **Standing to be vouched for.** Somebody's word, which is worth more than stones and is
  spent rather than kept - and which does one specific thing: it makes a house suspend its
  own admission bar for one person. See [Somebody's word, and the bar it
  skips](#somebodys-word-and-the-bar-it-skips), which is the mechanic this bullet used to
  gesture at without naming.
- **Access, in the sense that decides comprehension.** Being in rooms where things are
  practised, once the word has got somebody into the rooms.
- **Survivable risk.** The dangerous ruin is where the ceiling is broken - and it is
  survivable in proportion to what you walked in carrying. A well-supplied expedition into
  something lethal is a different act from a poor cultivator's.

None of that is a shortcut. It is the difference between a road being long and a road being
closed.

## Somebody's word, and the bar it skips

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="a child is being placed at a house whose bar they do not meet, or somebody asks how they got in" -->

**A favour buys one thing: it makes a house take a person it would otherwise refuse on the
bar.** Not money, not a recommendation that makes a good impression, not a discount. The
bar is not lowered and it is not waived by degrees - it is *skipped*, once, for one person,
and outside that one thing the word buys nothing at all.

This exists because the arithmetic without it is absurd, and everybody in the world would
have to explain the absurdity. A house states what it admits at. A child has no ordinal
until they have cultivated. So a child of the greatest family alive, at the age that family
places its children, qualifies for exactly the houses that refuse nobody - which makes the
greatest name in the province worth nothing at the one moment it should be worth
everything. A child of a house that keeps its own admission bar cannot enter *their own
family's house*, and waits in it.

The word is what makes a name mean something before the child has an ordinal. It is also
why a word from somebody at the top of the ladder is worth what it is, and why almost
nobody has one to give.

**The extreme case, and the case it is actually for, is a newborn.** A person with nothing
measurable about them except potential, taken now, against a stated bar they will not meet
for a decade and might never meet at all. The years that buys are not years of waiting.
They are years of teaching nobody else was going to give, in a house with a library, an
elder and a vein, beginning before the body has set - and beginning there young is not a
head start on the same ceiling, it is a different ceiling. What the house is taking in
return is a risk it cannot assess, which is why the price is what it is: it has no
measurement to go on and is betting on the asker's judgement rather than on the child.

**What a word does not reach.** Some bars do not move, and the reason is never
squeamishness - a bar that cannot be waived is one whose waiving would break something.
It would kill the applicant, or dissolve the thing the house runs on, or admit a
contribution the house has no use for. [`sects.md`](sects.md) and the catalog carry each
house's own reason in its own words. The negative case this file already states is the
sharpest one: the Hollow Court admits on evidence you could cross and *nothing else counts,
which explicitly includes being somebody's child*, and it is correctly the house where no
word works, because the arrangement inside it is what the bar is protecting.

And some bodies have no door for a word to skip. A posting is not an admission: people
stand there because they were appointed, by the apex above or by a house below, and a child
cannot be appointed. What looks like a very high bar from outside is not a bar at all. The
right instrument there is a nomination, which is a different thing with a different price.

> **Three different things are called a favour in this setting. Keep them apart.**
>
> - **This one** - the admission favour. Spent from outside a house, by somebody who is not
>   in it, on somebody who is not in it yet. It moves a gate.
> - **Internal patronage** - [`manuals.md`](manuals.md), "The chosen". A house that has
>   decided a disciple is worth it hands them the top of the shelf years before their rank
>   reaches it. That happens *inside* a house, to somebody already admitted, and how many a
>   house can favour is decided by how many copies of the top book it holds. It moves a
>   shelf, never a gate, and being favoured does not lift a manual's own entry requirement.
> - **The obligation** - a favour *owed*, in the sense [`items.md`](items.md) uses when it
>   lists one among the things worth holding. That is not an act, it is the record an act
>   leaves behind.
>
> The first and the third meet exactly once: spending an admission favour creates an
> obligation. They are cause and receipt, not the same thing.

## The one house where the word buys nothing

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="the Azure Cloud Pavilion's doors come up, or somebody offers to get a person in there" -->

The Azure Cloud Pavilion has **two doors, and only one of them is the door people come
through.**

- Its **membership** bar is the one everybody quotes, and it has never moved for anybody.
- Its **probation** door stands at the floor, and it is the only one of its kind in the
  world. The Pavilion will take an uncultivated mortal off the road, test them, and spend
  years finding out what they are.

Both are true at once, so the sentence to say out loud is: **it is already open and needs no
opening.** A favour buys nothing there because there is nothing to buy. You walk up. Being
handed the same child by somebody at the top of the world gets exactly what walking up the
mountain gets, and the Pavilion says so in the same words every time it is asked, on the
stated ground that a bar it moves once is not a bar.

There is a second half to this and it is the part that gets lost. The Pavilion's own
standing sits above what any family's name reaches, so nobody is ever *placed* there - a
child of the strongest house alive has not heard it named at home. That makes it
**simultaneously the most open door in the setting and the only one that cannot be opened
for anyone**, and the two halves are the same fact rather than a contradiction: the door
nobody can be let through is the door nobody needs to be let through.

It does not grant words either. It has the standing to move almost any bar and has never
once asked, on the stated ground that it would then be doing to another house what it
refuses to do to itself. That is a real cost, paid in a currency others spend freely. It
should read as admirable and faintly maddening at once, and it is not coldness - it is the
whole of what the house is.

A child placed at the Pavilion is therefore the one placement in the world where nobody is
carrying a debt. Depending on who you ask, that is either the cleanest thing somebody can do
for a child or a way of giving them nothing.

## Spending one on your own child

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="a cultivator with standing has a child to place, or a placed child asks who arranged it" -->

A favour is not only something that happened to somebody before they could talk. It is
**something a cultivator with standing may do, once they have a child**, and that makes it a
fact about relationships rather than only about where a run opened.

Start from what is *not* this. **Everywhere else, a cultivator raises their child in their
own house.** That is the ordinary case and it covers nearly the whole world. A sect is glad
to have an elder's child, it takes people who will make competent elders, and the bar for
that is one anybody's child might clear. No word is needed and none is called in. Reading
placement-by-favour as the general mechanism turns a sharp fact about a few houses into a
lazy explanation for every unusual placement in the world, and it is false.

What is left is narrow and it is where the drama is. Some bodies have no place for their own
members' children, by two opposite routes: one because of its **bar**, which only wants
people capable of the last realm, and the others because there is **no intake at all** and a
child cannot be appointed to a posting. Both produce a person of high standing whose own
institution has no room for their own child, and what follows from each is different.

The mechanics of the asking are the same wherever it happens:

- **The word runs through a person, never through an institution.** Somebody asks a friend,
  personally. Nobody writes to a house about a child, and no house writes as itself about
  one.
- **It is spent, not lent.** Standing goes out and does not come back, and what the asker
  takes on is an obligation that is deliberately never priced - naming a price makes it a
  transaction that ends, and this one does not.
- **It is visible to exactly one other person.** The identity of the asker goes to the
  friend being asked and to nobody else. The discretion needs no enforcement: naming them
  burns the namer's own face in front of exactly the people whose trust is their whole
  position.
- **The child is not told**, and the one person who could tell them will not. That refusal
  is honourable rather than cruel, and it will still be a refusal in a century.
- **It buys the gate and nothing else.** The child enters at the bottom of the receiving
  house's ladder like everybody else and climbs it the ordinary way.

So a child placed this way grows up with a placement they did not earn and cannot account
for, sometimes an unexplained memento, a good guess at the shape of it, and no name. The
memento is a clue and never a key: it cannot be shown to anybody who will answer, cannot be
traced through a register because nothing was written down, and cannot compel the friend who
took them in. What it can do is be recognised by somebody who has seen its like.

Where the parent's body is a posting rather than a bar, none of that secrecy applies -
appointments are public and the province has been reading the names for centuries. The child
knows exactly whose they are, and what they inherit instead of a mystery is an expectation
and a debt, in a house that took them in as a favour to somebody who will come back with
precedence and be in a position to repay it. Being visibly somebody's is a different weight
from being secretly somebody's, and it is not obviously the lighter one.

**And it is a gamble rather than a gift.** A child placed above their ability does not go
home, because unwinding the arrangement means one friend telling another that their child
was not enough, and nobody does that. They wash out into a servant's position and the house
keeps them - which is also why the answer to the asking is usually yes: if it takes, the
house has a strong member it did not have to find, and if it does not, it has somebody
loyal, well-connected, going nowhere and cheaper than anybody it could hire. The parent
placed them high precisely to avoid the bar that shut them out, and the receiving house has
a bar of its own. The risk was moved rather than removed.

That produces one of the better positions in this world to be a person from: somebody who
knows they were somebody's child, knows what they were supposed to become, has been inside
the place for decades being spoken over, and is on nobody's list of who matters.

## The shape of an immortal life

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="somebody asks what it actually takes to reach the top of the ladder" -->

The one path to the top of the ladder in the Late Age is a **conjunction**, and every term
is required:

```text
born somewhere that supplies the inputs
  + talent that was never guaranteed and cannot be improved
    + placement and a teacher, early
      + resources on a patriarch's scale, spent rather than hoarded
        + risk taken personally, repeatedly, in places that kill people
          + luck, which nobody arranges
```

Remove any one and the run ends short. That is why it is rare rather than merely
difficult, and why it stays legible: someone who reaches the last realm can point at every
term, and so can everyone watching.

**And the world knows it.** A cultivator who arrives at the top having been given
everything is not admired for the gift. What is remarked on is the part that could not be
given - the talent they happened to have, and the number of times they went into somewhere
lethal when they did not have to.

## The children of great houses mostly fail anyway

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="a great house's heir is failing, or the player resents somebody's birth" -->

This is the part that keeps it honest, and it must be true in the simulation rather than
merely asserted.

Most people born to enormous advantage still do not reach the top. They draw a muddled
root and the resources cannot fix it. They are placed well and comprehend nothing. They
are given a fortune and spend it on the wrong path for two centuries. They decline to go
into the ruin - which is the rational choice, and the reason the ones who went are talked
about.

So a privileged origin should be **visible in the run's opening position** and **not
visible in its outcome distribution**, except at the very top where it is one required term
among several. If being well-born reliably produces high-realm cultivators, the axis has
been implemented wrong.

## And it cuts the other way

<!-- tier: 2 trigger="the player has no house, no teacher, and no vein" -->

A poor origin is not merely harder; it is a different game. No teacher, no library, no
vein, and a hard local ceiling. What it does have is **ruins**, which do not check who your
parents were - the only door in the world that opens on nerve rather than on standing.

That asymmetry is the setting's own argument: the well-born climb by being supplied, and
the poor climb by being reckless, and the second road kills almost everyone who takes it.
