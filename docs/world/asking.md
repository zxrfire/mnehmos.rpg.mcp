# Asking: who you ask, and what you say

How information is actually obtained. Read with [discovery.md](discovery.md), which covers
how names arrive unbidden; this covers what happens when the player goes looking.

## The same question, four answers

Ask what the Sill is:

| Who | What happens |
|---|---|
| A carter, a farmhand, an outer disciple | **Genuine ignorance.** Not evasion. They have never needed the word and do not have it. They may guess, confidently and wrongly. |
| A shopkeeper who deals upward, a steward, a sect archivist | **They know, and they deflect.** A shrug, a change of subject, "that's not a thing to ask about here", or an answer so general it contains nothing. |
| The same person, after the right words | **Their manner changes before their answer does.** They reassess who they are talking to, and then tell you something real - usually less than everything, and usually with a price attached, even if the price is only that they now know you asked. |
| Your master, or someone who owes you | **A straight answer**, bounded by their own position. What they know, what they are permitted to say, and what it costs them to say it are three different limits, and all three apply. |

**Evasion and ignorance must be distinguishable eventually, and not immediately.** The
craft is that a deflection and a genuine blank should look similar the first time and
different the tenth. A player who has learned to tell them apart has learned something
real about the world.

## The right words

The second row becomes the third because of **what the player says**, not because of a
skill check.

The right words are things like: a name they should not know. A term used correctly.
Naming who sent them. A phrase that marks them as someone with business rather than
curiosity. Mentioning a debt, an obligation, or a third party who matters. Occasionally,
simply asking in the correct place, at the correct hour, having brought the correct thing.

Properties this must have:

- **It is not a persuasion roll.** It is not charisma, not realm, not a skill. It is
  whether the player said something that changes what the other person believes they are
  talking to. A Qi Condensation cultivator who uses the right term gets further than a
  Core Formation one who does not.
- **It is discoverable and specific.** The words exist somewhere - overheard, read in a
  ruin, given by a master, inferred from how someone else was treated. They are not
  guessable from first principles, and they are not a riddle with one solution.
- **Using them wrongly is worse than not using them.** Deploying a term you do not
  understand, to someone who does, marks you precisely - as an outsider who has been
  somewhere they should not have been, or as someone repeating what they overheard. Both
  are dangerous, and the second is accurate.
- **They are local and dated.** A phrase that opens a door in one province is noise in the
  next, and a phrase two centuries out of date identifies exactly where you learned it.

## Position decides what a straight answer costs

Even a willing master is bounded:

- **What they know**, which may be less than the player assumes and is often out of date.
- **What they are permitted to say**, by oath, by sect law, by an arrangement with
  something above them. A refusal here is not unwillingness and should not read as one.
- **What it costs them.** Telling a disciple about something above their stratum makes the
  disciple a person who knows it, and the master responsible for that. Some will do it
  gladly. Some will do it once and never again. Some will ask what the player intends to
  do with it, and mean it.

So the answer is shaped by the relationship, and the relationship is state the world
already tracks. A master who owes the player answers differently from one the player has
disappointed.

## Why this is the roguelike

**This is where the player, rather than the character, gets better.**

Every run begins in the same in-character ignorance: a new cultivator has heard of nothing
and knows nobody. But the person playing has learned, across runs, **who to ask, what to
say, and what a deflection looks like.** They know that the phrasing which opens a steward
in the Low Fall is useless in the Marches. They know which questions mark them. They know
that a particular kind of shrug means *I know and will not say* rather than *I have no
idea*.

That is the meta-progression, and it is entirely legitimate: nothing is carried by the
character, and everything is carried by the player. Death takes the cultivator, the
resources, the sect standing and the comprehension. It does not take the fact that you now
know what to say to a grain factor to find out who really holds the ford.

Design consequences:

- **Do not gate the right words behind an unlock flag.** If the player says the correct
  thing on their first run, in their first hour, it works. The barrier is knowing it, and
  that barrier is real without being enforced.
- **Do not randomise them per run.** Their stability across runs is the entire mechanism.
  A world that reshuffles its passwords teaches nothing.
- **Let the player be wrong for a long time.** Discovering that the polite question they
  have been asking for three runs is the one that makes people stop talking is a genuine
  and welcome lesson.
