# The spring that does not move

**Designed, not built.** The Burial Sands as it stands - the shows, the finders,
Wind Turn, the refused, and why nobody can hold any of it - is built and lives in
[`regions/the-blown-ground.ts`](../../../src/data/cultivation/regions/the-blown-ground.ts).
Read that first. This file is the layer that goes on top of it, and none of it is
in the catalog yet.

## What it is modelled on

**Dunhuang after the Tang.** When the court lost the western road in the 750s, the
oasis at the end of it was cut off from everything that had made it an outpost of
something larger. It fell to a foreign power for sixty years; then a local family
threw that power out and sent envoys back to the court the long way round, and the
court - which could no longer reach them - named their government a command of its
own and let them rule. For the next two centuries a local house governed the oasis in
the name of an authority that could not confirm it, answer it or remove it, married
into the courts around it, and ran the place as its own. Everything past the reach of
its water was nobody's.

What the design takes from it:

| Then | Here |
|---|---|
| A court that loses the road and never gets it back | An authority that once held the western road through the sands, cut off by whatever cut it |
| A local house ruling in that court's name | The **Moon Oasis Sect**, holding the spring under a grant nobody left alive can confirm |
| The crescent spring the dunes never bury | **The one fixed ground in the province** - a spring and a cliff, where everything else walks |
| The humming dunes around it | The sand sings when the wind crosses it; it is the sound the province is known by |
| A cliff cut with cave shrines over centuries, the rulers painted on the walls as donors | A cliff above the spring cut with meditation caves, each painted with whoever paid for it - a roll of who held the water, read by walking along it |
| A cave walled up against a threat and forgotten for nine hundred years | **A sealed cave** somewhere in that cliff, holding what was put away when the road was cut |
| Monasteries that held the grain and lent it at interest, on written contract | The **Bountiful Harvest Sect**, which lends grain against the season and keeps the paper |
| A crossroads of every people on the road | The refused, already designed, and everybody else who came up the road before it closed |

## Why it fits what is already built

The sands cannot be held because the thing that would be held - a show - has a
shorter life than any instrument that would hold it. **A spring is the exception, and
the exception is the whole design.** It does not walk. Rock does not walk. So there is
exactly one piece of ground in the province that outlives a grant, and whoever holds it
holds the only order there is.

That order stops where the water does. The Moon Oasis Sect cannot hold a show any more
than anybody else can, and does not try. What it holds is that **every finder who goes
out after a show has to drink**, and the only water that is always there is theirs. A
sect whose trade is carrying water is a sect that owns the one thing nobody in the
province can do without - which is why the owner's ruling that it is a sect and not a
caravan makes it stronger rather than stranger.

Lawless is therefore the right word and a precise one. There is law at the spring,
it is the sect's, and it runs as far as a day's carrying. Past that there is the
Burial Sands exactly as the catalog describes it.

## What it gives a player

- **A place that answers a letter**, in a province where nothing else does. The spring
  is where somebody can be found, owed, hired or asked.
- **Water as leverage.** Going out after a show means buying water or carrying it, and
  the sect knows who is out and for how long.
- **The painted cliff.** Walking it is reading the province's history off its own
  walls - who held the spring, who married whom, who was scraped off and painted over.
- **The sealed cave.** The largest find in the province, and a political one: what was
  put away when the road was cut may include the grant the Moon Oasis Sect rules
  under - which either proves its claim or ends it.
- **Grain on paper.** The Bountiful Harvest Sect's contracts are the one kind of
  obligation the sands keep, because they are kept at the spring and not on the ground.

## What building it would take

1. **One exception clause in the catalog.** `the-blown-ground.ts` derives that nothing
   is built because a wall is a dune by the next season. The spring and the cliff need
   to be stated as the single exception and why, or the catalog contradicts itself.
2. **A place row for the spring**, with the cliff and the sealed cave inside it as
   rooms, using the architecture that already exists.
3. **The Moon Oasis Sect seated there**, and its grant written as a claim whose truth is
   discoverable rather than stated - the same shape as `claimIsTrue` on a house's
   ancestor.
4. **The sealed cave as a site** with an opening condition, and its contents authored.
5. **The region's sensory bed** - the singing sand - on the `register` the narrator is
   about to be handed.

## Open questions

- **Which authority lost the road?** The catalog has apexes and courts; one of them
  should be the power that once held the western route. Pick one, or make it one that
  is gone.
- **Is Wind Turn at the spring?** The one market being at the one water is the obvious
  reading, but the catalog does not currently say where Wind Turn is.
- **The seat's name.** It was Sand Well, which now collides with the Burial Sands on
  "Sand". Its replacement has to pass the collision rule.
