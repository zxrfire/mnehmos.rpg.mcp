# The shape of play

What the game is, what a turn is, and who is on the page for it. Settled by the owner on
2026-09-24, while watching the narrator play on a local model. The narrator's rules that carry
this are in `src/web/prompt.ts` (`whoStaysOnThePage`, `theTurnToWrite`, `A_SITTING_IS_SHORT`)
and `src/web/the-narrator-plays-the-world.ts` (HOW TO PLAY A TURN, `aPersonsCard`).

## What the game is

> the game is dialogues, and between dialogues you move around. but obviously a lot of it is a
> role playing game

> an rpg game with a lot of talking and other events in text format

> in terms of like 30-50% talking, and then fighting, cultivating

A third to a half of play is the player talking to somebody. The rest is events: fighting,
cultivating, crossings, and moving from one conversation to the next. The prose is built for the
conversations first, and a change is judged by how they read.

> a turn is not ONLY conversation. it's also cultivation. but cultivation i'd say is pretty
> boring (which is fine, imagine it as something somebody spams) until they get interrupted by
> something. which is how it works today

A sitting is the act a player repeats. With nothing to break it, it is a few short lines: the
days going by, one detail of the place, what changed or did not. The engine interrupting it
(provisions gone, somebody coming over, the world arriving) is the turn, and gets the room.

> also make it so more places have just 1 person, this makes the game simplier i think

A place with one other person in it is a conversation waiting to happen. More places should be
like that than like a crowded square.

## Who is on the page

**Talking to one person is a one-to-one roleplay.** Only the two of you are on the page. Nobody
else speaks, and nobody else is described.

> if you're talking to an npc, no need to say what the other npcs say, they can just be in the
> background, no need to describe them too

> if i need someone else, the player ought to ask

Somebody else comes onto the page only when the player asks for them, when the engine puts them
in the turn (a ruling names them), or when a life turns and family is standing there. A death is
not a conversation, and the one who raised you is in the room.

**Talking to the room gets as many answers as the moment needs, never more than three.**

> if i talk to the room, 2-3 people respond, the rest are background noise

> 1 person CAN reply. it depends on what works best for the game. for "who am i" one person
> replying is good, cuz you don't NEED multiple

Three is a ceiling, not a target. The rest of the room is background noise: a line at most, and
nobody in it is named.

**It switches every turn.**

> and then i talk to 1 person and it turns 1 to 1 again (or talk to the room again)

Looking around, or any turn that is not a conversation, hands the whole room back.

## A face is not a name

> characters aren't randomly introduced with names. you don't know their name. you just see a
> jade beauty in red, or something. or wearing a sect uniform you don't recognize. (or you do,
> depending on perception)

> hide the name unless the player is sure this is them. cuz otherwise, they introduce
> themselves. and you can go like ohh you're x i've heard of you. and that gets rapport

> the player can try a random name too, and how they react is up to the npc

A person enters the prose as the player first sees them, built from their card: what they wear,
their age, what they are at, what their body shows. The name comes only when the player is sure:
they grew up with them, have met them, or the person gives it. A name only heard of is worth
holding, because it turns an introduction into recognition, and recognition into rapport. That
is an engine effect, not a line the narrator spends. A colour is recognised the same way, by
what the player knows.

Family is named by what they are to you, the word the engine decides from the tie: "your father,
He Ketao".

## How it feels

Emotion follows the stakes. Buying food is flat. A parting, a death, a crossing won or lost goes
all the way, the good and the bad alike, and the heavens do not soften it. Talk keeps the genre's
heat either way: quiet turns can exist, but most of the game is talking.
